Why rotate? On the real axis the integrand is pure phase e∓iω′x — it just spins and never settles, so the integral doesn’t converge. Tip the contour a quarter turn into the complex plane and that phase becomes a real decay e−ω′|x| (a Wick rotation) — now it converges. The catch: α and β must tip in opposite directions. As the ray swings around the branch point at x = 0, the power xiω/κ pays a real factor e∓φω/κ. At a full ±90° those factors are e±πω/2κ, opposite for α and β — and their ratio is the whole Boltzmann weight e−2πω/κ.
